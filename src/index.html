How to play

It's three things.

  1. Fly. Put your thumb on the circle in the bottom right and push the way you want to go — or just hold your thumb anywhere on the screen and she flies to it. Push all the way, or keep holding, to speed up. Pinch to zoom.Hold the mouse down and she flies that way. Keep holding to go faster. WASD works too. Scroll to zoom.
  2. Read. Get close to any rainbow and it opens on its own. No clicking.
  3. Leave one. Write one kind sentence. It turns into a rainbow that nobody else has, and it stays out here.

That's all you need. Everything below is extra, and you'll find most of it by accident.

If you want to know more

The colours mean something. Your words are turned into light. The coloured bar under each message is that sentence split into a spectrum, and the dark lines running through it are its actual letters. Type the same words twice and you get the same rainbow — always, on any device.

Twenty places to play. Scattered around are twenty coloured singularities. Fly into one and it drops you somewhere else for sixty seconds, with something different to do — sort light through a prism, hide from a pulsar's beam, ride a shockwave, hold three bodies in a line. Your best score in each is saved on your device. Nothing that happens in there can cost you anything out here.

Other cats. Nine of them, each a different colour, each carrying somebody's sentence across the field. Fly alongside one for a moment and she'll give you what she's carrying. They aren't other players — nobody else is online — they're just delivering the post.

Your own line. Every rainbow you read stays lit, joined up in the order you found them. Press ⤢ to pull right back and see the line you've drawn across the galaxy. It's yours; nobody else has that one.

The mouse. Now and then a mouse turns up, steals a message nobody has read yet, and runs off with it. While it has it, that message is genuinely gone. Catch the mouse and you get it back. It's quick, it weaves, and it will dive down the black hole to lose you.

The middle. There's a black hole at the centre, like there is in every real galaxy this size. It's the one thing you can see from anywhere, so head for it when you're lost. Fall in and it puts you back down somewhere else.

Tilt your phone. The ⌾ button lets you fly by tilting instead. Whatever angle you're holding it at when you turn it on becomes level. Touching the screen always wins while your finger is down.

Rare ones. About one message in forty comes out burning bright — a full, wide rainbow. They wear a halo and ring a high note, and a prism at the edge of the screen points toward the nearest one you haven't read.

Where they came from. Each message says. Founding means it was written to open the field so it was never empty. Everything else carries the day a person left it.
